## Detailed findings

### 1. Mapping, document type, and title-blind purpose — PASS

The official page 3 item under Indicator 9 asks for execution/process records demonstrating stakeholder participation in course design and review, including attendance, speaking, communication, approval, recognition, and support. The current Blueprint and crosswalk independently map that requirement to `DOCX 0042 = I09-E02 = checkbox 043`.

Page 1’s T01 quick-use guide and T02 mapping table establish who uses the document, what to record, and what the record should produce. Even with the title hidden, §§4–9 identify the work as tracking stakeholder participation against a specific course version, deciding whether each input is usable, tracing its disposition, and controlling approval and release. This is a suitable execution-record/sample-record genre rather than a course plan or interview-analysis report.

T02 and §1 distinguish the neighboring work:

- **0041 / I09-E01:** the course plan and how opinions are considered in design.
- **0043 / I09-E03:** demand-unit interviews and statistical analysis.
- **0044 / I09-E04:** full discussion, meeting, and email records and signed approval archive.

The candidate also says its sample is not a substitute for those neighboring deliverables. It stays within the scope of 0042.

### 2. Participation process and synthetic batch — PARTIAL

The process itself is substantial. Page 3 T04 presents a 90-minute sample agenda. Pages 3–4 T05 provides eight stages: define scope, build the stakeholder frame, invite, collect input, check quality, decide treatment, read back/revise, and authorize/archive. T06–T07 on pages 4–5 provide record fields and prompts separating attendance, speaking, support, approval, consent, version, source, disposition, owner, and deadline.

Pages 5–7 T08–T09 provide ten synthetic coded cases covering meeting attendance, oral and written input, phone summary, a structured response, withdrawal, nonresponse, and undelivered invitation. The document properly warns that invitation is not attendance and silence is not agreement.

Two details prevent a full completeness PASS:

1. **The sample event has no explicit date.** T04 on page 3 gives times from 14:00 to 15:30 but no event date, mode/location, or event ID. T08 on page 6 gives an invitation date, not the meeting date. Add a synthetic event date and an event identifier that links the agenda, attendance codes, and sample source index; register those synthetic facts.
2. **Three response channels have ambiguous event status.** T08–T09 on pages 6–7 describe P04’s written opinion as an “unsent sample draft,” P06’s phone summary as “not an actual call,” and P07’s form as “not actually issued,” yet count them among the seven valid opinions in T10–T11. Since the entire case is synthetic, these may be intended as hypothetical response events, but the current wording can also mean no response event occurred. State clearly that these are modeled hypothetical responses with no underlying source artifacts, or exclude them and recalculate if they represent non-events.

These gaps do not obscure the SAMPLE/SYNTHETIC status, but they reduce the usefulness of the batch as a worked execution record.

### 3. Flow calculations and course-time arithmetic

**Participation flow calculations — PASS.** T08–T11 (§6, pages 6–8) show:

- Invited 10 = delivered 9 + undelivered 1.
- Delivered 9 = responses 8 + delivered nonresponse 1.
- Responses 8 = valid opinions 7 + excluded 1.
- The invitee flow also reconciles: 7 valid + 1 excluded + 1 nonresponse + 1 undelivered = 10.
- Rates recompute correctly: 9/10 = 90.0%; 8/10 = 80.0%; 8/9 = 88.9%; 7/8 = 87.5%; and 7/10 = 70.0%.

The inclusion of P08’s withdrawal notice in the response count is explicitly explained; the withdrawn opinion content is excluded. P09’s nonresponse and P10’s undelivered invitation are distinguished.

**Course schedule — FAIL.** In §8, T13 on page 10, the instructional/assessment rows total 230 minutes in both versions:

- V0.2: 15 + 55 + 35 + 55 + 35 + 25 + 10 = 230.
- V0.3: 15 + 40 + 50 + 60 + 35 + 20 + 10 = 230.

But T13 then displays a 10-minute break before the teaching subtotal **and another 10-minute break after that subtotal**, while listing total time as 240 minutes. The displayed rows therefore total **250 minutes** for each version. T16 on page 11 states `230+10=240`, confirming that the intended design appears to include one break, not two. The technical receipt’s 240-minute result fails to account for both break rows.

**Required correction:** retain one 10-minute break per version, show it only once, preserve the 230-minute teaching/assessment subtotal, and recompute the 240-minute total. Reconcile the table and register after the correction.
